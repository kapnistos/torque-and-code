02 · Challenge

Lunar Landing Site Navigator

Difficulty: Intermediate to Advanced. Subjects: Moon, Human Exploration, Space Exploration, Software.

Missions to the lunar south pole work under hard limits on light and communication. The Sun stays close to the horizon, so ridges and crater rims can leave a site dark for days. Earth also dips below the horizon from some sites, which cuts direct radio contact.

Tools that compute Sun and Earth positions already exist, but they are hard to use. NASA asks for something intuitive: a way for mission planners, teachers and the public to compare landing sites and dates quickly, and see the power and communication picture at a glance.

All 2026 challenges on the Space Apps site

Will it have power?

How many hours of sunlight per day, how much a solar panel produces, and how long the longest dark stretch lasts.

Can it call home?

When Earth is above the local horizon, giving a direct-to-Earth communication window.

Which site, which date?

A ranked side-by-side comparison so the trade-off is obvious in seconds.

03 · What we build

From coordinates to a verdict

The heavy astronomy comes from NASA JPL's Horizons service. Our work is the engineering model on top and an interface that makes the answer obvious.

Step 1

Pick sites

Candidate south-pole sites with latitude and longitude, plus a date range.

Step 2

Query Horizons

Sun and Earth azimuth and elevation, seen from each site, hour by hour.

Step 3

Power model

Panel output from Sun elevation; battery needed to survive the longest dark gap.

Step 4

Comms windows

Hours with Earth above the horizon, and the longest blackout.

Step 5

Score & compare

Rank sites and dates; show two or three side by side.

P = A · η · 1361 W/m² · sin(esun)

A is panel area, η is cell efficiency, 1361 W/m² is the solar constant at 1 AU and esun is Sun elevation. This assumes a flat panel; a vertical, Sun-tracking panel (common at the poles) gets cos(e) instead, which we'll offer as an option.
